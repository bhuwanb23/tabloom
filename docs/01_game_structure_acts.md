# TABLOOM: The Last Mind of Aevum
### Document 1 of 5 — Game Structure: Acts, Connections & Gameplay

---

## How to Read This Document

Each act lists: **which reality it happens in**, **what the player actually does** (mechanics, puzzles, combat), **what story information it delivers**, and — critically — **which other acts it connects to and why**. The connections are the spine of the whole design: nothing here is a flat, linear act-1-then-act-2 structure. Information gathered in one act unlocks *interpretation*, not just access, in another.

---

## The Connection Map (read this first)

```
ACT I (Nara-0)
   ↓ plants the lie, plants the 15-shadows mystery
ACT II (Karth-Muun)
   ↓ plants the "dying soldier" doubt-seed
   ↘ (unresolved doubt carries forward to Act IX)
ACT III (Root Below — vision only)
   ↓ shows the dead Ari WITHOUT explanation
   ↘ (unresolved image carries forward to Act VIII)
ACT IV (Ora-Vell)
   ↓ gives the "invariant" concept + Veyr's grief
   ↗ RECONTEXTUALIZES Act II's doubt-seed
ACT V (Glass Eden)
   ↓ the almost-love, then its collapse
   ↗ USES emotional rawness Act IV built
ACT VI (Nara-0 revisit)
   ↓ memory-lock opens — but ONLY because Act IV taught the player what an invariant even is
   ↗ PAYS OFF Act I's 15-shadows mystery in part
ACT VII (Karth-Muun revisit)
   ↓ Pruning mastery finishes what Act II started
   ↗ PAYS OFF Kael's Act II line ("prize between us")
ACT VIII (Root Below, full)
   ↓ PAYS OFF Act III's vision in full — dead Ari explained
   ↗ REQUIRES Act VI's understanding of invariants to make sense
ACT IX (Heartwood Siege)
   ↓ Veyr fight; Veyr delivers the full truth
   ↗ COLLIDES Act II's doubt-seed + Act IV's grief + Act VIII's proof into one scene
ACT X (Heartwood — Endings)
   ↓ player choice, informed by everything above
```

The design principle: **every act plants at least one thing that only pays off two or more acts later.** This rewards a player who explores fully and pays attention to environmental/dialogue detail, and it means the "twist" in Act IX doesn't feel like it came from nowhere — it feels like something the player almost already knew.

## ACT I — "Ash and Rain" (Nara-0)

**Reality:** Nara-0 (rain-city, modern, melancholic)
**Playable Ari:** The Archivist

### Gameplay
- Tutorial act. Player learns basic movement, environment interaction, and the **tab-switch mechanic** (physically switching between two open realities mid-scene).
- First puzzle: the 15-shadows wall. Player must align light sources in the apartment to "match" one shadow correctly — teaches the game's core visual-logic puzzle language early.
- Root-craft unlocked: **Grafting** (basic) — used to mend a jammed memory-lock drawer that won't open yet (deliberately; it reopens fully in Act VI).
- First tab-switch puzzle: an action taken in Nara-0 (turning on a lamp) is shown to visibly affect Karth-Muun (melting part of a frozen gate) — teaches the "actions ripple across tabs" mechanic that all later puzzles build on.

### Story delivered
- The premise. The Shattering. Senn introduced. The lie planted softly ("keep looking, you may be surprised").
- Mirael (Nara-0 version) introduced as Ari's closest friend — establish warmth and history, NOT yet the confession.

### Connects forward to
- **Act VI** (the memory-lock drawer fully opens once player understands invariants)
- **Act X** (the "15 shadows, one body" image is quietly the whole story in miniature — worth a subtle visual callback in the ending cinematics)

## ACT II — "The Man Who Chose Well" (Karth-Muun)

**Reality:** Karth-Muun (frozen war-loop battlefield)
**Playable Ari:** The Oathblade

### Gameplay
- Combat-focused act — first real boss-adjacent encounter (a looped enemy squad that resets on a timer, teaching the player that not everything in this world can be permanently defeated yet — only *paused*, which mirrors the loop-curse itself).
- Root-craft unlocked: **Pruning** (basic) — used to sever one section of the frost-curse, freeing a small area but not the whole loop (full resolution saved for Act VII).
- Puzzle: navigating a battlefield that resets every few minutes requires the player to plan actions that "persist" across resets — an early lesson in the invariant theme, taught mechanically before it's taught narratively.

### Story delivered
- Kael Orin introduced. His and Mirael's relationship established as genuine, unforced.
- The confession subplot deepens — Ari watches love that simply *worked*, elsewhere.
- **The doubt-seed:** a dying, minor NPC soldier delivers the "careful who tells you what you want to hear" line. Deliberately easy to miss or dismiss.

### Connects forward to
- **Act IV** (Ora-Vell recontextualizes this line completely — it stops sounding like battlefield delirium)
- **Act VII** (returning here to finish the Pruning arc and permanently end the loop)
- **Act IX** (this is the first time the player heard anything resembling "someone is lying to you," and Act IX is where that finally gets a name: Senn)

## ACT III — "A Glimpse, Uninvited" (The Root Below — vision sequence)

**Reality:** The Root Below (accessed involuntarily, not through normal travel)
**Playable Ari:** Ambiguous — the vision doesn't fully commit to one face

### Gameplay
- Short, disorienting, non-combat sequence. Player has limited control, movement feels "wrong" (delayed input, drifting camera) to sell the hallucination framing.
- No puzzle to solve here — this act is deliberately about **denying the player agency**, mechanically reinforcing that Ari isn't in control of what he's seeing.
- Ends abruptly — the vision "tears" and cuts to black before the player can approach the dead Ari fully.

### Story delivered
- First glimpse of the Root Below and the dead-Ari image, with zero explanation.
- Senn's first visible crack — the "nothing that's happened yet" non-answer.

### Connects forward to
- **Act VIII** (this entire sequence is replayed, recontextualized, and completed — same location, same body, full explanation, and this time the player has full control)
- Should visually rhyme hard with Act VIII's version so the player's memory does the connecting work, not exposition.

## ACT IV — "The Father Who Catalogued a Death" (Ora-Vell)

**Reality:** Ora-Vell (golden hospital-empire, Veyr's origin world)
**Playable Ari:** The Patient / Machine Saint (hybrid — Ari is recovering from an in-world injury here, giving a narrative reason for a slower, exploration-heavy pace)

### Gameplay
- Investigation-focused act, closest to a detective/environmental-storytelling structure. No major combat — deliberately a breather after Act II's boss fight, before Act V's emotional gut-punch.
- Puzzle: reconstruct a timeline from scattered medical logs and ritual transcripts (a physical in-world puzzle — arranging documents in the correct chronological order reveals the hidden final entry).
- Root-craft: no new ability, but the **concept** of an invariant is explicitly taught here via an in-world diagram/relic the player examines — this is the "tutorial" for the game's central theme, disguised as lore.

### Story delivered
- Veyr's daughter, her repeated death, the birth of the Nullroot concept.
- The word "invariant" enters the story's vocabulary for the first time.

### Connects forward to
- **Act II** (retroactively — the dying soldier's line now reads as foreshadowing, not delirium)
- **Act VI** (the player now has the conceptual tool needed to understand what the Nara-0 memory-lock is protecting)
- **Act IX** (Veyr's full grief, established here, pays off in his final confrontation — he's not a stranger by then)

## ACT V — "The Reality That Almost Lied Perfectly" (Glass Eden)

**Reality:** Glass Eden (false paradise)
**Playable Ari:** unspecified — Glass Eden doesn't have a "job" identity, because it isn't a real place; Ari appears here closer to his true self than in any other reality, which should be reflected in more naturalistic animation/dialogue

### Gameplay
- Emotionally the heaviest act, mechanically the gentlest — mostly walking, dialogue, and a slow-paced puzzle where the player must recognize which elements of the environment are "too perfect" (a match-the-flaw puzzle in reverse — find what's WRONG about a world with no wrongness).
- Root-craft: Grafting is used here in a narrative-mechanical dual-purpose way — the player literally "grafts" a piece of truth into the illusion to unravel it, a puzzle that is also a metaphor the game doesn't explain, just lets happen.

### Story delivered
- The almost-love. Mirael-of-Glass-Eden's real line: "You were never unworthy, Ari. My heart simply did not go where your heart waited."
- Senn's second visible crack (the "holding a door shut" beat).

### Connects forward to
- **Act X, Ending One** specifically — this act's central line is the thematic seed of the true ending's final Ari/Mirael exchange.
- **Act VIII** — emotionally prepares the player for the dead-Ari reveal by having already shown them what happens when Ari gets *close* to the thing he wants and it turns out hollow.

## ACT VI — "What Memory Gives Back" (Nara-0, revisit)

**Reality:** Nara-0, returned to
**Playable Ari:** The Archivist, changed — should visibly read as more weathered than Act I's version (lighting, posture, animation set)

### Gameplay
- The Act I memory-lock puzzle reopens, now solvable — but the solution requires applying the "invariant" concept from Act IV, not a new skill. This is a deliberate callback puzzle, not a new mechanic, to reward players who remember Act I clearly.
- A short vision/memory-playback sequence (player has partial control, similar to Act III but this time coherent rather than disorienting) shows a fragment of the Witness Mind's own memory: the search across candidates, the finding of Ari's invariant.

### Story delivered
- The player (and Ari) learn *why* Ari specifically was chosen — not narrated by Senn, but discovered directly. This is a critical structural choice: **the player should learn this before Senn ever admits it**, so that Senn's continued silence in Acts VII–VIII reads as active complicity, not simple unawareness.

### Connects forward to
- **Act VIII** (this act supplies the conceptual key; Act VIII supplies the horrifying proof)
- **Act IX** (this is the moment the player starts actively distrusting Senn, well before Ari voices it)

---

## ACT VII — "Breaking the Loop" (Karth-Muun, revisit)

**Reality:** Karth-Muun, returned to
**Playable Ari:** The Oathblade, changed

### Gameplay
- Pruning mastery act — a genuine boss fight (the strongest combat encounter so far), fighting the "curse-heart" sustaining the frost-loop. Uses full Pruning stance-play established in Act II.
- Victory permanently changes this reality's state for the rest of the game (a visible, walkable, thawed battlefield in all future visits/cutscenes set here) — mechanical payoff should feel large and irreversible, contrasting the "reset" feeling of Act II.

### Story delivered
- Kael's payoff line: "Some things are supposed to end once and mean it" — delivered here, watching the loop end for good.
- This line is deliberately positioned as thematically loaded but not yet flagged as important — its full weight only becomes clear when Ari repeats a version of it to Veyr in Act IX.

### Connects forward to
- **Act IX** (Ari's climactic rebuttal to Veyr is a direct callback/evolution of Kael's line here)

---

## ACT VIII — "The Proof He Wasn't Ready For" (The Root Below, full)

**Reality:** The Root Below, entered properly (not a vision)
**Playable Ari:** neutral/composite — this is the one act where the game can justify blending visual elements of all 5 Ari versions, since the Root Below is "outside" normal reality-logic

### Gameplay
- Full exploration of the graveyard of the ten dead branches — the game's most atmospheric, least-guided level, deliberately open and quiet after seven acts of tighter pacing.
- Puzzle: navigating decayed root-matter requires switching rapidly between the invariant-logic learned across all previous acts — effectively a "final exam" level that reuses every mechanic taught so far (Grafting, Pruning, tab-switch ripple logic) in combination.
- The Act III vision sequence is directly revisited — same location, same dead Ari, but now fully rendered, fully explorable, and readable (the carved explanation the vision denied the player before).

### Story delivered
- Full reveal: this dead Ari is from a branch where the invariant broke — Mirael once chose him, and the paradox tore him apart.
- Senn's most honest line of the whole game: "I knew what happens if it doesn't happen, too. I chose the version where you kept walking." Still not the full truth (the Mind's motive for keeping Ari hoping remains unstated) — but the closest to honesty Senn gets before Act IX forces the rest out.

### Connects forward to
- **Act IX** (this is the loaded gun; Veyr fires it)

---

## ACT IX — "What Veyr Knows That Ari Doesn't" (Heartwood Siege)

**Reality:** The Heartwood (the tree itself — the game's only reality that isn't a "branch")
**Playable Ari:** all versions converge — mechanically, the player may get brief moments controlling different Ari "aspects" mid-fight, reflecting the convergence

### Gameplay
- The game's central boss fight: Veyr Sol Auric, full Nullroot moveset. Combat design should force the player to alternate Pruning and Graft stances fluidly (unlike earlier acts, which taught them somewhat separately) — mechanically this is the culmination of everything taught in Acts I, II, IV(concept), VI, VII.
- Structurally a two-phase fight: Phase 1 is a genuine, fair skill-based boss fight. Phase 2 begins only after Veyr is defeated — he doesn't attack again, but delivers the truth as a scripted, non-combat sequence, offering Ari the Nullroot. This is a critical design choice: **the emotional climax must NOT be gated behind combat difficulty** — every player who beats the boss gets the same story beat, ensuring nobody misses the game's thesis due to skill gap.

### Story delivered
- Full truth: the invariant, why Ari was chosen, the Root Below's dead Ari confirmed as a warning the Mind buried, and the sharpest reveal — the Mind had structural incentive to want Ari's heart to keep breaking.
- Veyr's death, and his final offer of the Nullroot to Ari.

### Connects forward to
- **Act X** — everything gathered across nine acts is now active in the player's hands for the final choice.

---

## ACT X — "Three Ways to Finish a Sentence" (The Heartwood — Endings)

**Reality:** The Heartwood
**Playable Ari:** full convergence

### Gameplay
- No combat. A structured choice sequence — NOT a simple dialogue-wheel binary, but a short sequence of smaller choices/actions across the final level (what the player does with the Nullroot physically, what they say to Senn, whether they approach the Mind directly) that combine to determine which of the three endings triggers.
- This avoids a cheap "pick ending from a menu" feeling — the ending should feel earned through action, consistent with the rest of the game's puzzle-through-action storytelling.

### Story delivered
- Ending One: The Regrowth (true ending)
- Ending Two: The Merge (tragic mirror)
- Ending Three: The Sundering (transcendent/costliest)

## Design Notes for Whoever Builds This

- **Pacing rule:** Acts II, VII, and IX are your combat-heavy acts. Acts IV, V, and VIII are your exploration/emotion-heavy acts. Acts I, III, VI, and X are shorter connective/revelatory acts. This alternation is intentional — don't let two heavy-combat acts sit back to back for a hackathon build; if you must cut, cut a connective act, never two heavy acts in a row.
- **For a hackathon MVP:** if you can only build 2–3 acts, build **Act I (tutorial/hook), Act II (first real gameplay+combat), and a condensed Act IX/X (the emotional payoff)** — this gives judges the mechanic, the stakes, and the theme in miniature without needing the full 10-act arc.
